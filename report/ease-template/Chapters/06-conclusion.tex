\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper asked what retrieval strategy, if any, best supports LLM-based
detection of malicious PyPI packages. On the dataset of
Ibiyo et al.~\cite{ease2025rag}, we compared seven \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}
pipelines that differ only in retrieval strategy: code-level retrieval
improved detection over the no-retrieval baseline (malicious-class F1 from
0.888 to 0.97), sparse BM25 performed comparably to dense retrieval at a
fraction of the infrastructure cost, and contrastive retrieval reduced false
alarms. In contrast, behavior-summary queries underperformed the baseline,
suggesting that query construction, not retrieval itself, was the limiting
factor. As a side exploration, the typed-decision model Jev performed
competitively without retrieval but did not reach the strongest retrieved
configuration, and the residual failures we documented---metadata-cloned
typosquats and false positives on security-adjacent benign
packages---indicate that pipelines restricted to \texttt{setup.py} may
benefit from name-level signals and additional evidence.
