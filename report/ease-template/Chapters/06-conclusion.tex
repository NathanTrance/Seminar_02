\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper asked what retrieval strategy, if any, best supports LLM-based
detection of malicious PyPI packages. Using the dataset of
Ibiyo et al.~\cite{ease2025rag}, a fixed \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}
generator, and a code-only knowledge base, we compared a zero-shot baseline
against dense, sparse, hybrid, behavior-based, and contrastive retrieval.

For the controlled Llama suite, three conclusions emerge: code-level
retrieval is a large win (malicious-class F1 from 0.888 to 0.97, mostly
through recall); plain BM25 matches a 4B embedding retriever within 0.4 F1
points; and contrastive retrieval cuts the false-positive rate to 0.1\% at
0.948 recall. Reducing queries to AST behavior summaries is counterproductive
(F1 0.878), suggesting that query construction, not retrieval itself, was the
limiting factor in our setting.

As a side exploration, the typed-decision model Jev performs competitively
without retrieval (F1 0.960) but does not reach the strongest retrieved
configuration. The residual failures we documented---metadata-cloned
typosquats and false positives on security-adjacent benign
packages---indicate that pipelines restricted to \texttt{setup.py} may
benefit from name-level signals and additional evidence.
